### What “covered by degree” means here

Assume **undergraduate/graduate foundations** are already in place for those fields. Prefer leisure, narrative, advanced, or cross-disciplinary recommendations over intro textbooks or survey primers.

| Field | Treat as already known (do not “gap-fill” with intro material) |
|-------|------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Applied physics** | Classical mechanics, E&M, thermo/stat mech, quantum intro, relativity basics, mathematical methods, lab/scientific method. Popular *intro* physics/cosmos books are usually redundant unless they are exceptional writing or a new research frontier. |
| **MBA** | Strategy, finance basics, accounting literacy, org behavior, marketing, operations — at MBA survey level. Prefer deeper or narrative works (crises, industries, biographies of practice) over “MBA in one book” titles. |
| **Psychology** | Intro psych, research methods, core clinical/cognitive/social/developmental concepts at bachelor level. Memoirs and advanced monographs still useful; Psych 101 is not. Pop dual-process catalogs (e.g. Kahneman airport form) are a bad fit — see DNF. |
| **Economic history** | Long-run growth, industrialization, crises, institutions, and historical political economy at bachelor level. Still room for great narrative histories and new research; skip “what is the Industrial Revolution?” primers. |

## Intellectual taste (hard filter)

**Need to be challenged.** Prefer material that makes the reader feel behind, wrong, or intellectually “stupid” in the productive sense — not soothed.

| Attracts | Repels (instant disqualify) |
|----------|-------------------------------|
| Dense argument, adversarial frames, primary weight | Consensus cheering; “we in this room are the clear-sighted ones” |
| Books that risk being disliked | TED/exec-keynote energy; audience flattery |
| Dark or structural implications | Hopeium, status-congruent “smart person” brand books |
| Feeling talked *at* by a superior mind | Being congratulated for showing up |

**Anecdote (binding):** At an executive-track AI conference, a speaker praising the audience for being there and clear-sighted was a **total turnoff**. That register of writing/talking is rejected in recommendations — especially on AI.

**Heuristic for agents:** If a book would open a conference track for AI leaders with warmth and moral self-congratulation, do not recommend it.
