# The Age of Reason

**Author:** [[Thomas Paine]]  
**Finished:** 2008-04-28  
**Domains:** [[Philosophy MOC|Philosophy]], [[Classics MOC|Classics]]

## My review *(imported from calendar)*

When Thomas Paine feared for his life in 1793, in the middle of the French Revolution, he decided to put in writing what he had planned for a long time - his thoughts on the topic of religion. His fear of arrest or worse was valid as the first part was bairly finished before Paine was arrested. Part two is a more detailed discussion that Paine wrote after his release from prison.

It would be nothing but fair to mention Paine's own beliefs. Paine was a deist. He discredits the bible in The Age of Reason by using the bible itself as proof of its inaccuracy. But he was a firm believer in god. Paine saw god in nature, in its supposed creation, and was of the opinion that the bible itself discredits god's reputation.

The book is highly entertaining, interesting and educational. It is filled with references to the bible and the reader may look up any verse used by the author in the arguments. I used that possibility several times, and always found the verses in their context within minutes. I can say nothing than that the book is a valuable read for theists and atheists alike! It is also an important work in liberty and freedom of speech, perhaps best highlighted by the first quote below.

> You will do me the justice to remember, that I have always strenuously supported the Right to every man to his opinion, however different that opinion might be to mine. He who denies to another this right, makes a slave of himself to his present opinion, because he precludes himself the right of changing it.
> Dedication. p vii.

> [The church] has set up a religion of pomp and revenue in pretended imitation of a person whose life was humility and poverty.
> p 42.

> It has been by wandering from the immutable laws of science, and the light of reason, and setting up an invented thing called "revealed religion", that so many wild and basphemous conceits have been formed of the Almighty.
> p 194.
