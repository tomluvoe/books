### What “covered by degree” means here

Assume **undergraduate/graduate foundations** are already in place for those fields. Prefer leisure, narrative, advanced, or cross-disciplinary recommendations over intro textbooks or survey primers.

| Field | Treat as already known (do not “gap-fill” with intro material) |
|-------|------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Applied physics** | Classical mechanics, E&M, thermo/stat mech, quantum intro, relativity basics, mathematical methods, lab/scientific method. Popular *intro* physics/cosmos books are usually redundant unless they are exceptional writing or a new research frontier. |
| **MBA** | Strategy, finance basics, accounting literacy, org behavior, marketing, operations — at MBA survey level. Prefer deeper or narrative works (crises, industries, biographies of practice) over “MBA in one book” titles. |
| **Psychology** | Intro psych, research methods, core clinical/cognitive/social/developmental concepts at bachelor level. Memoirs and advanced monographs still useful; Psych 101 is not. |
| **Economic history** | Long-run growth, industrialization, crises, institutions, and historical political economy at bachelor level. Still room for great narrative histories and new research; skip “what is the Industrial Revolution?” primers. |

## Reading-log caveats

1. **Vault start:** Leisure reading is systematically logged from the Books Google Calendar **2005 onward**. Earlier books (especially **1999–2005 studies**) may be missing or only backfilled when remembered.
2. **Degree reading ≠ vault entries.** Course books and academic reading were not logged as calendar “books finished.”
3. **“Gap” in a MOC** often means *not logged as leisure reading*, not *never encountered*.
4. **Exact dates** for retrospective backfills may be approximate (`date_precision: approximate` on the note).

## How agents should use this

- **Librarian:** Cross-check `Context.md` before claiming “never read X” for physics/psych/MBA/econ-history basics.
- **Recommender:** Weight **narrative nonfiction, literature, geopolitics, markets stories, and advanced/cross-field** works; avoid remedial lists in degree fields.
- **Curriculum builder:** Build on degree foundations; fill *vault* gaps (e.g. Russian literature next to autocracy reading), not *syllabus* gaps.
