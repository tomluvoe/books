# Rights of Man

**Author:** [[Thomas Paine]]  
**Finished:** 2007-08-05  
**Domains:** [[Philosophy MOC|Philosophy]], [[Politics & Society MOC|Politics & Society]], [[Classics MOC|Classics]]

## My review *(imported from calendar)*

Rights of Man is a two part reply to Reflectons on the Revolution in France by Edmund Burke.

In the first part Paine defends the French and the American revolutions and argues for human liberty and equality. The primary impediments on liberty and equality are maintained to be a hereditary government and an absence of a constitution.

The second part offers suggestions on how to reform the British government and ideas on how America, France and Britain can cooperate to the benefit of all three countries. Paine puts forward his ideas of government; in many ways a liberal system, but also socialist as he argues for a progressive tax system.

Rights of Man is very interesting and forwards very compelling arguments. It stirred quite a controversy in Britain due to the direct criticism of the then current social institutions and Pain was subsequently put on trial for seditious libel. It is a great read, and interesting both from a political and historical point of view.
