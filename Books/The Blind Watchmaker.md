# The Blind Watchmaker

**Author:** [[Richard Dawkins]]  
**Finished:** 2007-06-06  
**Domains:** [[Science & Skepticism MOC|Science & Skepticism]]

## My review *(imported from calendar)*

In 1802, William Paley used the watchmaker analogy as an argument for the existence of God; more specifically that as nature exhibits complexity and thus looks designed, it must have had a designer. Dawkins borrows the name of that analogy for the title of a book that describes how it is possible for nature as we know it to exist without an intelligent creator, that is, to have been created by a blind watchmaker.

The Blind Watchmaker explains and argues for evolution by means of natural selection. It also refutes many of the opposing arguments, for example the complexity of the eye. Dawkins shows the design flaws in the eye, the many different types of eyes that have evolved under different natural settings and how they have done so, in a very pursuasive manner.

The book was originally published in 1986, but it feels surprisingly current as science, mostly in the form of biology, is possibly under even more threat today than then. The only reasonble explanation for the unwillingness to accept evolution is religion; as scientifically, there is no doubt of the validity of the theory. The uneasiness among people exposed to the theory must have its root in the realization that humans are, at the core, no different from apes or any other living entity. And that we are ourselves just one step in the tree of life and will no doubt evolve into a different species over time.

But uneasiness or not; feelings do not affect whether something is true or false! Dawkins makes with The Blind Watchmaker a very compelling and convincing case for the theory of evolution by means of natural selection. A must read for anyone who is alive.
