# Extraordinary Popular Delusions

**Author:** [[Charles Mackay]]  
**Finished:** 2007-11-10  
**Domains:** [[Finance & Markets MOC|Finance & Markets]], [[Psychology MOC|Psychology]], [[Classics MOC|Classics]]

## My review *(imported from calendar)*

Extraordinary Popular Delusions is a popular history book by Charles Mackay. Written in the first half of the 19th century, it investigates and describes a number of moral epidemics that have inflicted themselves on the masses.

Included within the covers are descriptions and analysis of the economic bubbles of the Mississipppi Scheme in 1720, the South-Sea Bubble in 1711 and the Tulipomania in 1634 - not very unlike those of more recent times. Investigations into the pseudosciences of the alchemists, magnetisers, fortune-tellers and modern prophecis. In depth accounts and historical descriptions of the underpinnings for religious undertakings such as the Crusades and the Witch Mania.

Extraordinary Popular Delusions is a very impressive book. It is not possible to understate that. On one hand, it is a captivating popular history book with descriptions of historical events and undertakings that everyone should be familiar with. It is of uppermost importance for our liberty and our common future that people know the history and risks of the follies of mankind. On the other hand, it is a brutal and accurate debunking of many pseudosciences - that are still alive today even though they were shown to be false so long ago. Written more than 150 years ago, the book is as relevant and imporant today as it was then. A must read!
