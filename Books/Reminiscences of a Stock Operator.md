# Reminiscences of a Stock Operator

**Author:** [[Edwin Lefevre]]  
**Finished:** 2006-06-21  
**Domains:** [[Finance & Markets MOC|Finance & Markets]], [[Classics MOC|Classics]]

## My review *(imported from calendar)*

Reminiscences is the stock market classic by Edwin Lefevre published in 1923. It is the first-person account of the life of the "fictional" trader Larry Livingston known in real life as Jesse Livermore, who is still one of the most famous traders of all time. The story follows Livingston from a young age and his first job, posting stock quotes in Boston, to day-trading in bucket-shops and later on his way to become one of the most famous characters on Wall Street. Livermore made and lost several fortunes during his life and the book holds many of the valuable lessons he learned thoughout this journey.

I have an affection for memoirs and I really enjoyed Reminiscences. The book does not span Livermore's whole life, but ends as he was on his financial peak in the early 20s. In real life his fortune later turned and he eventually ended his life with a suicide. While the book is a vivid description of a great speculator who at his peak kept a whole fleet of Rolls Royce limousines and lived life to the fullest, it is also an interesting insight into stock speculation in the early 1900s.
