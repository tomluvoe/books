# The End of Faith

**Author:** [[Sam Harris]]  
**Finished:** 2007-05-09  
**Domains:** [[Science & Skepticism MOC|Science & Skepticism]], [[Philosophy MOC|Philosophy]]

## My review *(imported from calendar)*

The End of Faith makes the case against any belief expressed from irrational or dogmatic ideas.

The book collects its empirical data from history and analyzes that data in the light of reason, ethics and moral philosophy. In the process the book gives a fairly interesting description of the history of religion. In the latter chapters the book turns to morality and ethics in general, and presents a controversial defence of torture and the ethical necessity of violence as a form of defence. The latter part has unsurprisingly met strong critizism which the author has responded to on his web site. In the last chapter the book considers spiritualism and the author discusses eastern mysticism, consciousness and the sense of self. Also this part has met critizism.

I personally found the book interesting, quite educational and somewhat frightening. I am not very enthusiastic about the spiritual part and it is easy to believe that it is at odds with the argumentation in the first part, but strictly it might not be. I have not made up my mind about that completely. The ethical necessity of torture and violence in defence situations are two very difficult discussions in moral philosophy. It comes around to similar arguments, such as if it is morally defensible to kill one person to save two. I would recommend the book to anyone who would like a discussion about religion and morals.
